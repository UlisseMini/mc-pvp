Replay checklist

Read the exchange in this order

  1. Find the new hit. A click before it may have slowed the old velocity. The new knockback replaces that velocity.
  2. Check the first tick after it. Look for a selected target, local sprint, and a clear miss cooldown.
  3. Check your feet and the launch. The floor gives you stronger forward acceleration and more drag. Jumping before impact gives up that grounded move. A stronger packet can explain most of a bigger slide by itself.

In a close trade, practice keeping your crosshair on the opponent through the hit and getting W back down early enough to be sprinting at contact. Time the click around the trade. The 35 ms opening in the first clip is too short to wait for the knockback and then react.

What the full sample showed

In the 64 cases that lost their sprint brake, we checked the first six moves for unclicked openings and in-range aim misses. In that comparison, 34 had only an unclicked opening, 11 had an opening before a later aim miss, four had only an aim miss, and 15 had neither. An opening means a click could have braked your movement locally. Server damage is a separate question.

Across all 80 slides, we swapped the recorded factors between each pair of hits and averaged each factor’s effect over swap orders. Losing the brake explained the largest share in 46 cases. Ground and jump state led in 18, packet speed in 15, and other movement inputs in one. Restoring W while keeping the same contacts would have saved a median of 0.08 blocks.

These were selected big slides from 68 games. Each slide was at least 2.5 blocks and at least 0.75 blocks farther than the previous trade over the same number of moves.
