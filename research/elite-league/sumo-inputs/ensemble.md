# Pooling the decisions of four trained MLPs

The primary experiment found across-seed variation among competent policies and a severe loss against learned opponents when own velocity was removed. This exploratory follow-up uses only policies with their existing own velocity, hurt cues and opponent displacement. It asks whether pooling several independently trained decisions can make a stronger opponent. The earlier six-clock scores motivated this hypothesis and are development evidence for it. The new test clocks below are reserved.

Consider the three seeds of base8, edge8 and full8, plus the original eight-unit titleholder. Enumerate all 210 distinct four-member sets. Select on exactly the original development panel and original score (field mean, weakest matchup and W-tap, equally weighted). Resolve exact ties by lexical order. Each member has equal weight. Concatenate the hidden units and average the five action scores before applying the original physical action thresholds. This compiles exactly into the existing 32-unit feedforward policy. Compare it to averaging the parameters of the very same four members into an eight-unit network and to the development-best individual in that four. No extra perception or latency information is available to any constituent. Check equivalence of the compiled policy on a common sequence of local observations.

Freeze the three selected networks before opening the clock donors. Take four future exogenous host timing schedules from the native single-feature timing-removal vs W-tap fixtures, both seats and asymmetric RTT pairs. No combat outcomes enter the simulator. Evaluate on the ten original opponents and all 257 historical entries. Score the original ten across four RTT pairs and the archive across the two asymmetric pairs. Compare to the incumbent and plain W-tap on the same settings.

Then run the pooled actor, parameter average, best member, incumbent, and plain W-tap on the live 1.8.9 server against W-tap, the incumbent and shift-check-v4-4 in both seats and at both asymmetric RTT pairs (60 independent bouts). Use the existing native rank rule. Publish the winner as a separate duel bot. The earlier global title follows its independently frozen promotion rule. Restrict this study to the stated roster, including any losses, and report it with the other ablations.
